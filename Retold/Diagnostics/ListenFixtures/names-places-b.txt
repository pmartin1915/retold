My grandmother Imogene Thibodeaux grew up outside Opelousas, Louisiana, in a little house on Bayou Teche. Her brother Thaddeus fixed outboard motors for the shrimpers, and every summer my mother and her cousin Philomena took the train from Tuscaloosa to stay with them. Grandma Imogene called everybody sugar, even the mailman, a tall quiet man named Ezekiel Broussard. When she finally moved to Muscle Shoals to live with my aunt Dorothea, she brought a cast iron skillet, a jar of cane syrup, and a photograph of the church in Grand Coteau where she was married.
